## 1. Which states require all-party consent, statute citations, and does it cover video calls and transcription without saved audio?

### Takeaway
About 10–13 states require consent from everyone in the conversation, depending on how hybrid states are counted. The core list is California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Washington. Connecticut, Michigan, Nevada and Oregon are hybrid or disputed. Most of these statutes ban the *interception or acquisition* of a communication's contents, not just storing a recording. So "transcribe live, keep no audio" is not a reliable safe harbor, and no appellate court has squarely ruled on it.

### Cited Findings
- No authoritative count exists. One recording-law guide counts 9 states as all-party for every call and conversation plus 5 that split the rule by medium. Another counts 12. Counts differ because hybrid states are classified differently. — [RecordingLaw (vendor/legal-info site, "2026")](https://recordinglaw.com/united-states-recording-laws/); [Justia 50-state survey](https://justia.com/50-state-surveys/recording-phone-calls-and-conversations) (could not fetch)
- Commonly listed all-party states: California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Washington. Connecticut, Michigan, Nevada and Oregon are most often treated as hybrid or borderline: Connecticut has an all-party civil rule for phone calls, and Nevada's Supreme Court has read its statute as all-party for wire communications. — search summary of [RecordingLaw](https://recordinglaw.com/united-states-recording-laws/) and [Kilpatrick Townsend, "Wiretap Laws in the United States" (July 2024)](https://ktslaw.com/Insights/Alert/2024/7/Wiretap-Laws-in-the-United-States)
- Citations confirmed in the search sources: California Penal Code §§ 631 (wiretapping/interception) and 632 (eavesdropping on/recording confidential communications); Illinois 720 ILCS 5/14-2 through 5/14-4; Washington RCW 9.73.030. — [RecordingLaw](https://recordinglaw.com/united-states-recording-laws/); [Justia survey](https://justia.com/50-state-surveys/recording-phone-calls-and-conversations)
- Video and online meetings: a court has applied CIPA §631 to an AI notetaker joining online meetings (Otter, below). Courts are treating video-call audio as a communication covered by these statutes. — [In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation, N.D. Cal. No. 5:25-cv-06911 (Crandall Consulting case page)](https://crandall.consulting/website-tracking-cases/in-re-otter-ai-privacy-litigation/)
- Transcription without saved audio, Massachusetts: secretly capturing someone's words with AI devices "may violate" the Massachusetts wiretap statute even when no audio file is saved. No reported Massachusetts appellate decision has decided whether temporary audio processing that produces a text snippet or summary is an "interception." A 1976 case holds that a recording is not an interception when all parties know about it. — [Serpa Law (Boston criminal defense firm blog), 2026](https://www.serpalaw.com/boston-criminal-law-updates/ai-wearables-massachusetts-wiretap-law/)
- Transcription without saved audio, New Hampshire: NH's statute covers "aural or other acquisition" of contents, so a law professor quoted by CNBC (Oct 18, 2026) believes on-device audio-intelligence features could qualify as interception even without recording. — [CNBC, "New AI wearables push the limits of privacy and wiretap laws" (2026-10-18)](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/18/ai-wearables-privacy-wiretap-laws.html)
- General point: many wiretap statutes focus on *acquisition/interception of content*, not on keeping a recording. A Florida-focused guide says that keeping only text instead of audio does not remove the all-party consent requirement. — [CNBC (2026-10-18)](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/18/ai-wearables-privacy-wiretap-laws.html); [neoteo.com explainer (secondary)](https://www.neoteo.com/en/ai-wearables-can-raise-wiretap-questions-without-saved-audio)
- Unverified pending item: one search result mentions a California bill "SB 690" signed September 30, 2026, which reportedly relates to CIPA's application to commercial uses. I could not verify it. — [search result via recordinglaw.com/brasstranscripts](https://brasstranscripts.com/blog/state-by-state-recording-laws-2026-guide) (vendor source; UNVERIFIED)

### Inferences
- A meeting copilot should treat *any* real-time capture of another party's speech, including transcription with audio discarded, as covered in all-party states. It needs affirmative notice plus consent (or a documented consent mechanism) from every participant, not just the user.
- Because the strictest participant's location can control, a cross-state call should default to the all-party rule. Government meetings often include out-of-state contractors.
- The "recording indicator always visible" rule in AGENTS.md lines up with the "all parties know" defense cited for Massachusetts. Showing the indicator only to the user does not inform the other parties, so the tool also needs an in-meeting announcement or a bot name/chat message.

C.S. § 5703/5704; Del. Code tit. 11 § 2402 vs. § 1335 (conflicting). Verify each before use.

- SB 690 (California) status and contents are unverified.

## 2. Recent lawsuits about AI notetakers under wiretap/eavesdropping and biometric laws, and what they mean for live-only or on-device tools

### Takeaway
The leading case is *In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation* (N.D. Cal., No. 5:25-cv-06911, filed Aug 2025 as *Brewer v. Otter.ai*). It reportedly survived a motion to dismiss on August 13, 2026 on ECPA, CIPA §631 and BIPA voiceprint claims. The court's key theory is that a vendor that *retains recordings and trains its own models on them* is plausibly a "third-party eavesdropper" rather than an extension of its customer. Separate BIPA class actions target Fireflies.AI's speaker recognition. Tools that keep no data and do not train on it are better placed under this theory, but they are not immune.

### Cited Findings
- *In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation*, No. 5:25-cv-06911 (N.D. Cal., Judge Eumi K. Lee). Order dated **August 13, 2026**, at the pleading stage:
  - Survived: federal Wiretap Act (ECPA), CIPA § 631, both Illinois BIPA voiceprint claims, UCL, and unjust enrichment.
  - Dismissed, many with leave to amend: CFAA, California CDAFA, the Washington privacy-act claim, and some intrusion and constitutional privacy claims.
  — [Crandall Consulting case page](https://crandall.consulting/website-tracking-cases/in-re-otter-ai-privacy-litigation/); [RecordingLaw news (2026)](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/otter-ai-wiretap-lawsuit-explained/); [Lawsuit Intelligencer](https://lawsuitintelligencer.com/otter-ai-cipa-ruling)
- Reasoning reported in commentary: Otter is plausibly a third-party eavesdropper under CIPA §631 because it keeps recordings and uses them to train its own models. That retention and training take Otter outside the line of cases treating a vendor as its client's extension. This is not a liability finding, and Otter denies unlawful interception. — [basilai.app (vendor blog, 2026-09-03)](https://basilai.app/articles/2026-09-03-otter-ai-august-13-ruling-vendor-training-third-party-eavesdropper.html); [basilai.app (2026-10-15)](https://basilai.app/articles/2026-10-15-otter-ai-third-party-eavesdropper-ruling-ai-notetaker-buyers.html). - No ruling after August 2026 was found. — same sources
- *Cruz v. Fireflies.AI Corp.* (Illinois federal court, reported Dec 2025). Venue is reported inconsistently (C.D. Ill. No. 3:25-cv-03399 vs. N.D. BIPA voiceprint class action. — [Top Class Actions (2026-03-19)](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/lawsuit-news/fireflies-ai-sued-over-alleged-unlawful-data-collection-from-meeting-participants); [complaint PDF via Workplace Privacy Report (Apr 2026)](https://www.workplaceprivacyreport.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/938/2026/04/Fireflies.ai-Complaint-1.pdf)

- BIPA can apply if *any participant is physically in Illinois* when their voice is captured, regardless of where the vendor or the other participants are. — [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/illinois-bipa-suit-targets-ai-note-takers-practical-lessons-for-meeting-transcription)
- Commentary on cloud notetakers (Otter, Fireflies, Zoom AI Companion): they create permanent, searchable vendor-held records, which raises discovery and privilege exposure. — [Mayer Brown, "AI Notetakers: Productivity Tool or Emerging Legal Risk?" (June 2026)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/ai-notetakers-productivity-tool-or-emerging-legal-risk) (search summary only); [Blank Rome](https://www.blankrome.com/news-and-events/when-ai-takes-notes-protecting-privilege-privacy-and-professional-obligations/)
- Search results also name Granola as a target of 2026 notetaker suits, but I found no case details. — [BuildBetter blog (vendor)](https://blog.buildbetter.ai/ai-notetaker-lawsuits-2026-how-to-record-meetings-legally/) (UNVERIFIED)

### Inferences
- **Third-party theory:** As reported, the Otter theory turns on the *vendor's own use* of the data (retention plus training). A product that does no training on customer data, keeps no audio, and processes as the customer's agent fits the "extension of the user" line of cases better. Those are product rules already in AGENTS.md: no trains_on_data providers for data_class "meeting".
- **Live-only limits:** "Live-only" does not fix the *consent* problem in all-party states (Q1). It mainly reduces third-party-eavesdropper and retention risk. The backend and LLM providers also receive the audio or text in real time, so their contract terms (no training, no retention) matter.
- **Speaker enrollment:** Speaker enrollment, diarization tied to named identities, or "speaker recognition" is the BIPA trigger in the Fireflies suits. Anonymous diarization ("Speaker 1/2") is lower risk than voiceprint matching, but whether diarization embeddings count as "voiceprints" is unsettled.

### Gaps
- I did not read the Otter August 13, 2026 order. Claim list, reasoning and leave-to-amend deadlines come from secondary sources that are partly inconsistent.
- No outcomes yet (MTD, settlement) in the Fireflies cases.
- No court ruling found on a purely on-device or ephemeral transcription tool.

## 3. State laws and executive orders on state agencies' own AI use, including AI transcription and meeting tools

### Takeaway
No state statute specifically regulates AI meeting notetakers in government. The rules come from state CIO policies, several of which do address notetakers directly. Georgia bans third-party AI note-taking bots in state meetings. Missouri bars them from sensitive meetings unless enterprise-approved. Arizona, Wisconsin and North Carolina guidance treats AI transcripts as public records. Statewide frameworks (Texas TRAIGA, New York LOADinG Act, California EOs, Colorado's replacement AI law, Pennsylvania and Virginia EOs) add disclosure, procurement and inventory duties.

### Cited Findings
**Notetaker-specific state policies**
- **Georgia (GTA):** Requires prior GTA authorization for generative AI tools used regularly, including transcription and note-taking tools. Third-party AI note-taking tools are *prohibited* in State of Georgia Microsoft Teams meetings or any other conferencing platform. Built-in Teams recording and transcription is allowed. Recordings and transcriptions become public records subject to retention. — [Georgia Technology Authority, "Guidance for State Organizations"](https://ai.georgia.gov/guidance/guidance-state-organizations) (search summary; page date not seen)
- **Missouri (OA policy SP-19):** AI meeting transcription and notetaking bots are barred from sensitive meetings unless the tool is a state-approved enterprise service. AI-generated drafts, including unused ones, are subject to retention schedules and may be responsive to Sunshine Law requests or discovery. — [Missouri OA, SP-19 Acceptable Use for AI](https://oa.mo.gov/sites/default/files/SP-19%20Acceptable%20Use%20for%20Artificial%20Intelligence.pdf)
- **Arizona (ADOA Statewide Procedure 2000PR, Use of Generative AI, March 2024):** Transcriptions are public records under Arizona public records law. Advises against automated transcription where confidential or personal information is discussed. — [ASET/ADOA PDF (2024-03)](https://aset.az.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/2000PR%20-%20Use%20of%20Generative%20AI%20Procedure.pdf)
- **Wisconsin (DET Acceptable Use Policy, effective 2025-03-10):** Unless agency policy expressly permits otherwise, users must keep recordings and transcripts for the applicable retention periods. — [Wisconsin DET AUP](https://det.wi.gov/Documents/AUP%20-%20Effective%20Date%203.10.2025.pdf)
- **Maryland DoIT:** Reportedly has guidance on AI-powered call recording and transcription tools. — [ClerkBase (vendor)](https://clerkbase.com/ai-meeting-transcripts-public-records) (UNVERIFIED; primary not found)

- For state-agency customers, the binding constraint is usually the agency's CIO policy and approved-tool list, not a statute. - California N-5-26 procurement standards and NY LOADinG impact assessments could become vendor obligations in state contracts.

## 4. Biometric privacy laws (Illinois BIPA, Texas CUBI, Washington) and voiceprints / speaker enrollment

### Takeaway
All three statutes name voiceprints. BIPA is the main risk because it has a private right of action with $1,000/$5,000 liquidated damages. Since the 2024 amendment, damages are counted per person rather than per scan, and the Seventh Circuit held in April 2026 that this applies retroactively. Texas CUBI is AG-enforced only, but with large penalties (Meta paid $1.4B in 2024). Any speaker-enrollment or voice-ID feature needs prior written (electronic OK in Illinois) consent and a public retention and destruction policy.

### Cited Findings
- **Illinois BIPA (740 ILCS 14):** Public Act 103-0769 (SB 2979), signed **Aug 2, 2024**, limits repeated same-method collection to one recovery per person and adds "electronic signature" to "written release." — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2024/08/illinois-bipa-biometrics-law-amended-for-damages); [Greenberg Traurig (Aug 2024)](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/8/bipa-update-illinois-limits-liability-and-clarifies-electronic-consent-for-biometric-data-collection)
- The Seventh Circuit held on **April 1, 2026** that the amendment applies retroactively to cases pending on Aug 2, 2024 (Clay v. Union Pacific). — [ABA Business Law Today (May 2026)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2026-may/7th-circuit-holds-bipa-damages-remedy-applies-retroactively/); [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/ph-privacy/7th-circuit-confirms-bipa-amendment-has-retroactive-application)
- BIPA voiceprint claims against Otter survived dismissal (Aug 13, 2026), and Fireflies faces two BIPA voiceprint class actions (see Q2). — [Crandall Consulting](https://crandall.consulting/website-tracking-cases/in-re-otter-ai-privacy-litigation/); [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/lawsuit-news/fireflies-ai-sued-over-alleged-unlawful-data-collection-from-meeting-participants)
- **Texas CUBI (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 503.001):** Covers voiceprints along with retina/iris scans, fingerprints and hand/face geometry. Bars capture for a commercial purpose without informed consent. The AG has exclusive enforcement, with civil penalties up to $25,000 per violation and no private right of action. — [Texas AG CUBI page](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/file-consumer-complaint/enforcing-privacy-and-data-security-laws-protect-texans/biometric-identifier-act); [Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/class-action-insider/texas-ag-dont-mess-with-texans-biometric-info.html)
- The Meta CUBI settlement was **$1.4B (July 30, 2024)**, which the AG calls the largest ever obtained by a single state. The AG has also sued Google over face and voice data. — [Texas AG CUBI page](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/file-consumer-complaint/enforcing-privacy-and-data-security-laws-protect-texans/biometric-identifier-act); [Texas Lawbook](https://texaslawbook.net/how-metas-1-4b-settlement-impacts-your-obligations-under-cubi/)
- TRAIGA (effective Jan 1, 2026) amended CUBI, reportedly including a narrow AI-training exemption. — [Security Industry Association (2025-06-24)](https://www.securityindustry.org/2025/06/24/groundbreaking-texas-ai-law-also-brings-needed-clarity-on-use-of-biometric-technologies-for-security/); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/texas-enacts-ai-consumer-protection-law/) (details unverified)
- **Washington:** The Washington privacy-act claim against Otter was dismissed with leave to amend (Aug 2026). — [RecordingLaw news](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/otter-ai-wiretap-lawsuit-explained/)

### Inferences
- Speaker enrollment ("learn my voice", named-speaker matching across meetings) is the highest-risk feature. It should be opt-in per person with a written/electronic release, include a published retention and destruction schedule, and be disabled by default for participants who have not consented. In practice that means non-users.
- Government agencies are often exempt from BIPA (state and local units of government are excluded from "private entity"). The *vendor and contractors* are not, so a copilot serving an agency still carries BIPA exposure.

## 5. Public records and open-meetings laws: are AI transcripts of state/local government meetings public records?

### Takeaway
Yes, generally. State guidance consistently treats AI recordings, transcripts, summaries and even unused AI drafts as public records subject to retention schedules and disclosure. Content and purpose control, not where the record is stored or whose account holds it. Closed or executive sessions are the main hazard because a third-party bot can capture confidential content. I found no court ruling specifically on AI meeting transcripts.

### Cited Findings
- **North Carolina:** No AI-specific statute exists. A UNC School of Government analysis says an AI tool that records or transcribes a meeting may have created a record subject to disclosure under NC's Public Records Act (G.S. 132) unless an exception applies. The content and purpose of a record, not its location, decide its status. — [UNC SOG Coates' Canons, "The Intersection of AI and the Public Records Act" (2026-03-11)](https://canons.sog.unc.edu/blog/2026/03/11/the-intersection-of-artificial-intelligence-and-the-public-records-act/) (search summary only)
- **Georgia, Missouri, Arizona, Wisconsin:** Each treats AI meeting recordings or transcripts as public records subject to retention and disclosure (Missouri: including unused AI drafts under the Sunshine Law). — see Q3 sources: [GTA](https://ai.georgia.gov/guidance/guidance-state-organizations), [MO SP-19](https://oa.mo.gov/sites/default/files/SP-19%20Acceptable%20Use%20for%20Artificial%20Intelligence.pdf), [AZ 2000PR](https://aset.az.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/2000PR%20-%20Use%20of%20Generative%20AI%20Procedure.pdf), [WI DET AUP](https://det.wi.gov/Documents/AUP%20-%20Effective%20Date%203.10.2025.pdf)
- **Washington (MRSC):** The Municipal Research and Services Center reportedly advised local governments to "consider the public records implications" before adopting AI notetakers. AI outputs of public meetings may be public records even in a council member's personal account. — [eScribe (gov-meeting software vendor) blog](https://www.escribemeetings.com/blog/ai-meeting-mintes-vs-ai-notetakers/) (secondary; MRSC primary not fetched)
- **Minutes vs. AI summaries:** Statutory minutes must record attendance, quorum, motions, seconds and votes. Generic AI summaries do not satisfy that. Closed-session discussions are confidential under most open-meetings laws, and a third-party bot recording one is a key exposure for clerks. — [eScribe](https://www.escribemeetings.com/blog/ai-meeting-mintes-vs-ai-notetakers/); [ClerkBase](https://clerkbase.com/ai-meeting-transcripts-public-records) (both vendors)
- Cloud notetaker outputs are discoverable business records. — [Coblentz Law](https://www.coblentzlaw.com/news/its-okay-to-say-no-to-ai-notetaking-and-meeting-recordings/); [Blank Rome](https://www.blankrome.com/news-and-events/when-ai-takes-notes-protecting-privilege-privacy-and-professional-obligations/)

### Inferences
- For government users, the copilot is effectively a records-creation system. It needs export, retention-schedule support, legal hold, and no silent auto-deletion that could breach retention law. This pulls against "live-only / don't store" designs: an agency may be *required* to keep what the tool creates, so a "no retention" mode should be a configurable, agency-controlled policy.
- Open public meetings usually have no expectation of privacy, so wiretap consent is less of an issue there than in internal agency meetings or contractor calls. Executive or closed sessions need a hard "do not capture" mode.
- Contractors' notes of meetings with agencies may become agency records if shared or held on the agency's behalf. That varies by state and is unverified.

### Gaps
- No court ruling or AG opinion was found that specifically holds AI meeting transcripts to be public records, or exempt.
- Primary MRSC and Maryland DoIT guidance were not retrieved. Indiana guidance claimed by ClerkBase is unverified.
- No state-by-state survey of open-meetings laws on recording executive sessions with AI was found.
- No sources found on whether *ephemeral* (never-stored) AI transcripts count as "records." Most definitions turn on a record being "made or received," so this is open.
